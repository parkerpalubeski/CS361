## Facebook's internal motto for a while was "Move fast and break things". This is definitely a sentiment rooted in Agile.
### What are some of the benefits and drawbacks of adhering to that motto? Give potential examples.
Rapid software deployment is the chief benefit of this approach, and I imagine Facebook had adopted it because in their early days, there was a strong emphasis on absorbing market share of the growing social media industry and to beat out competitors. In such an environment, deploying products as quickly as possible is incredibly important. It could also allow developers to push their ideas into development quickly, and refine them rapidly through user feedback rather than extensive testing. However, this also multiple drawbacks. The accumulation of technical debt by years of adherence to this method is likely extreme. And if a product launches with bugs, security flaws or other flaws that would have been prevented with a more conservative approach, the product may suffer from poor reception in the critical post-launch period, or may even lead to things like legal action/regulatory fines.
### Facebook no longer uses that motto. They changed it to "Move fast with stable infrastructure." Speculate on why.
I imagine that this had to do with many of the drawbacks above. Legal fees and regulatory fines make up a massive chunk of expenses for their company, and plenty more I'm sure is spent trying to avoid said expenses with teams dedicated to legal compliance, oversight, etc. As their company has become larger, the scale and cost of failures has also become so great that strong foundations to their software is truly necessary. And since none of their core businesses (Social media, AI, Advertising, etc.) are without competition, initial reception is also critical.

## The exploration talks about cases where an overbearing Agile process gets in the way of getting work done. Imagine and describe a specific case.
A company adopts a performance metric that is measured by commits to GitHub rather than overall impact. In such a case, engineers performing something like a well-vetted, complex refactoring to improve maintainability and scalability might be viewed as a failure by management, while engineers who take up basic, meaningless tasks and commit frequently may not be viewed as such. Thus the agile process may in some cases actively punish engineers who are making a meaningful impact, while rewarding ones who don't.

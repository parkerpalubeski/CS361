## What are some potential hazards to allowing an LLM to select and complete To-Do items on its own?
If an LLM completes a task incorrectly, but believes that it's correct, it may move on to new issues based on the failed output of the previous task, corrupting the rest of the codebase in turn. There are also some risks that it may violate critical business constraints like modifying core infrastructure with a hallucination. Without human review, it may also fail to explain/justify its code changes.

## Even if Scrum-style sprints begin to get in the way of development, brainstorm how they might still be worth keeping even when you're working at LLM speeds.
Scrum might still be worth keepin in some aspects because Scrum put a lot of emphasis on planning and broad systems design, which is exactly what is important to ensure that an Agent behaves and doesn't go off the rails, as well as ensure that whatever the LLM is building actually meets client requirements and isn't just designed to pass tests quickly.

## Where do you personally fall on the LLM scale of "never look at the code" and "always have an engineer understand all the code"?
While I wouldn't consider myself one against embracing LLMs, I am a strong believer that an engineer should understand what their code does, where it fits into the project, and how it is meant to function. An engineer is not only tasked with delivering reliable code, but they are accountable for it when it fails. When a product fails, the end-users don't blame an LLM as much as the engineer who signed off on it entering the codebase.
So from a scale of 1-10 (1 being never look at the code), I would consider myself somewhere around an 8 to 9.

## For the previous question, what are the benefits and drawbacks of the two extremes?
### Never look at the code
#### Benefits:
* Maximum velocity
* More focus on broader systems design
#### Drawbacks
* Any errors/edge cases enter production without human verification

### Always Have an Engineer Understand All of the Code
#### Benefits:
* Maximum Trust
* Deep knowledge in the team for when LLMs fail

#### Drawbacks
* Low Velocity
* High Overhead
* No human can be reasonably expected to comprehend a multi-million line code base

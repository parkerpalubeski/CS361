## The official Scrum process spells out a lot of detail for how a sprint progresses and who is involved. But here I've described a looser, more general process. What do you imagine are the benefits and drawbacks of sticking with the real Scrum process as opposed to using a variant?
### Benefits:
* Scrum gives clearly defined goals and time slices, letting a team focus in on a specific item for a fixed duration
* Allows for high adaptability to changing requirements
* Continuous maintenance of the process (checking in, fixing bottlenecks, etc.) makes for easy improvement
### Drawbacks:
* It may be easy to overestimate, and even easier to underestimate, just how much effort a task will take. This could lead to an inconsistent pace where some sprints are easy and some are absolutely grueling.
* Constant meetings produce overhead, especially unnecessary in a process that already is working well

## If it's a small team and the Product Owner and Team Lead are the same person, what conflict of interests might arise?
A Product Owner and Team Lead may in some cases have fundamentally different goals. A Product owner is incentivized to deliver the product quickly, and ultimately to satisfy the client over anything else. A Team Lead meanwhile is more incentivized to push for realistic deadlines and protect the Developers from burnout. And when both of these roles are occupied by the same person, they are unable to point to the person in the other role to blame for failures in their own, and have to accept responsibility for both.

## The Retrospective is split out from the Review and Demo. Describe a hypothetical scenario where the Review and Demo went well but the Retrospective did not.
As the scope of a project increases and the team is pushed to add additional features that weren't part of the original plan, the team has to cut corners in order to meet client demands. The client is shown a complete product and loves it, but behind the scenes (in a way the client is unable to comprehend) the code is now a fragile, unscalable, unmaintainable mess of spaghetti code. Because the client is happy, Demo and Review went well, even if retrospective is largely negative.

## The three stand-up questions help the manager keep things on track. How do they also help the developers?
They could help the developers by alerting management to where tough points have arisen that weren't predicted previously, so they can allocate new resources/manpower towards solving that problem. 

## Why is it okay (or even better) to keep Backlog items in the distant future deliberately vague?
Because priorities and requirements can change. Items or certain features contained within an item may be thrown out at a moment's notice, so keeping things vague protects the team from wasting valuable hours writing design docs or specs for a feature that ultimately will never be implemented.

## What might happen if every developer had their own definition of "done"?
Inconsistent quality would be the most prominent consequences. Certain code might not be vetted as carefully and the codebase quality would vary widely depending on *who* wrote the feature.
